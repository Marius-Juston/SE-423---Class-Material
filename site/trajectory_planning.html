A* Search

Finds the least-cost path using f(n)=g(n)+h(n). Each cell is expanded at most once (closed set); stale queue entries are skipped.

f(n) = g(n) + h(n) g = cost-so-far, h = heuristic

4-conn: cardinal moves, cost 1. Heuristic = Manhattan |Δr|+|Δc|.

8-conn: + diagonals, cost √2. Heuristic = octile:

h = max(Δr,Δc) + (√2−1)·min(Δr,Δc)

Both are admissible and consistent for their move sets. Diagonal moves may not cut the corner of a wall (both orthogonal neighbours must be free).

Turn-and-Drive: state (row, col, θ), starting at θ_start. Cost = move length + θ-pen·|Δθ|, plus the final turn θ-pen·|θ_goal − θ| at the goal — better for diff-drive.

Hybrid A*

Expands kinematically feasible arcs from continuous poses (x,y,θ): v = 1 cell/s, ω ∈ {−ω_max … ω_max} (Steers values), arc length L, integrated exactly (see Kinematics below).

Closed set = grid cell × 15° heading bin (24 bins). Goal reached when within 0.8 cell and |θ − θ_goal| < θ tol.

Analytic expansion: at the start and every 10 expansions, compute the shortest Reeds-Shepp path (min radius r = v/ω_max) to the exact goal pose and collision-check it every 0.05 cell. If it is free the search ends and the path finishes exactly at the goal. RS paths may reverse (light-blue dashes).

Lyapunov (Kanayama) Controller

eₓ = cos(θ)·Δx + sin(θ)·Δy eᵧ = -sin(θ)·Δx + cos(θ)·Δy eθ = θ_r − θ (Δx = x_r − x, Δy = y_r − y) v = v_r·cos(eθ) + Kx·eₓ ω = ω_r + v_r·(Ky·eᵧ + Kθ·sin(eθ)) ω_r = κ·v_r (path curvature × ref speed)

Kx→longitudinal Ky→lateral Kθ→heading. The reference pose (x_r,y_r,θ_r) moves along the path at v_ref (orange ring).

For a feasible, continuously moving reference with v_r > 0 and Kx,Ky,Kθ > 0, V = ½(eₓ²+eᵧ²) + (1−cos eθ)/Ky is a Lyapunov function (V̇ = −Kx·eₓ² − v_r·Kθ·sin²eθ/Ky ≤ 0), so the tracking errors converge to zero (Kanayama et al., 1990).

Grid paths have heading jumps at corners, so they are corner-smoothed (Chaikin) into the light-blue reference first. On reverse Reeds-Shepp segments v_r < 0 and the Kθ term uses |v_r|.

Diff-Drive Kinematics

ẋ = v·cos(θ) ẏ = v·sin(θ) θ̇ = ω

Exact update over Δt for constant (v, ω) — the robot moves on a circular arc of radius v/ω:

x' = x + (v/ω)·[sin(θ+ωΔt) − sin(θ)] y' = y − (v/ω)·[cos(θ+ωΔt) − cos(θ)] θ' = θ + ω·Δt (ω = 0: x' = x + vΔt·cosθ, y' = y + vΔt·sinθ)

Used by both Hybrid A* and the simulator (no forward-Euler drift). Nonholonomic: cannot move sideways; can spin in place or drive straight.

Dubins Curves

Shortest path for a forward-only car with minimum turning radius r (Dubins, 1957). The optimum is always one of 6 words:

CSC: LSL RSR LSR RSL CCC: RLR LRL L = left (CCW) arc R = right (CW) arc S = straight

Each word has a closed-form solution (Shkel & Lumelsky, 2001). Compute every word that exists and keep the shortest; in the optimal CCC the middle arc exceeds π·r.

Reeds-Shepp Curves

Also allows reverse gear; switching gear (a cusp) costs nothing. Reeds & Shepp (1990) showed the optimum is one of 48 words in 9 families (C|C|C, CC|C, C|CC, CSC, CCᵤ|CᵤC, C|CᵤCᵤ|C, C|Cπ/2SC, CSCπ/2|C, C|Cπ/2SCπ/2|C; "|" = cusp).

Notation: + forward, − reverse e.g. L+ R− L+ (fwd-left, back-right, fwd-left)

Every Dubins path is also a valid RS path, so the RS length is always ≤ the Dubins length.

Green = forward, orange = reverse, ◆ = cusp. Solver: port of OMPL's ReedsSheppStateSpace. Used for analytic expansion in Hybrid A*.

Turning Circles

At pose (x,y,θ) the car can turn left on the circle centred at (x − r·sinθ, y + r·cosθ) or right on the circle centred at (x + r·sinθ, y − r·cosθ). Every arc of a Dubins/RS path lies on a circle of radius r tangent to the path.

Why Polynomials?

Represent trajectories as continuous functions. Motors need smooth profiles — no instantaneous jumps in velocity or torque.

Cubic (3rd order)

x(t) = c₀ + c₁t + c₂t² + c₃t³

4 coefficients → constrains position & velocity at endpoints. Cannot constrain acceleration (the ẍ₀/ẍf sliders are disabled): ẍ(0) and ẍ(T) are whatever the fit produces, so acceleration may jump at the segment endpoints.

Quintic (5th order)

x(t) = c₀+c₁t+c₂t²+c₃t³+c₄t⁴+c₅t⁵

6 coefficients → constrains position, velocity, and acceleration. Guarantees smooth acceleration.

Motor Limits

Max RPM → v_max. Limited torque → a_max.

Red shading = limit violations. Try increasing T or reducing endpoint constraints.

Jerk Minimization

min J = ∫₀ᵀ x⃛(t)² dt

For a single segment with position, velocity and acceleration fixed at both ends, the Euler–Lagrange condition is x⁽⁶⁾ = 0, so the minimum-jerk trajectory is exactly the quintic above (Flash & Hogan, 1985, rest-to-rest). A numerical QP is only needed for multi-segment paths through waypoints or with inequality limits (v_max, a_max) — and is fast enough for MCUs.

Elastic Band

The path is a chain of points pᵢ between fixed start and goal — a rubber band pulled taut by internal tension, pushed off obstacles, and optimised by gradient descent (Quinlan & Khatib, 1993).

This demo keeps the time step ΔT between points fixed. A true Timed-Elastic-Band (TEB, Rösmann et al., 2012) also optimises every time interval ΔTᵢ (and the poses' headings), which is what lets it enforce velocity/acceleration limits exactly and minimise travel time.

Obstacle Repulsion

F_obs = k_obs·(1/d² − 1/ρ₀²)·n̂ (d < ρ₀)

d = clearance from pᵢ to the obstacle edge (clamped ≥ 0.2), n̂ = unit vector away from the obstacle centre, ρ₀ = 1.5 (shaded halo). ∝ 1/d² close in, falling smoothly to 0 at ρ₀.

Smoothness (tension)

F_sm = k_s·(pᵢ₋₁ − 2pᵢ + pᵢ₊₁) = −∂/∂pᵢ [ (k_s/2)·Σⱼ‖pⱼ₊₁ − pⱼ‖² ]

Gradient descent on the sum of squared segment lengths — the band's elastic tension. It pulls each point toward the midpoint of its neighbours (shortening and straightening the band).

Kinematic Limits

Max velocity: with fixed ΔT = 1, ‖pᵢ₊₁ − pᵢ‖ ≤ v_max·ΔT; longer segments are contracted with gain k_vel.

κᵢ = |turn angle at pᵢ| / mean(ℓᵢ₋₁, ℓᵢ)

Min turn radius: where κᵢ > 1/r_min, pᵢ is pushed toward its neighbours' midpoint (reducing the turn) in proportion to the excess curvature. Both limits are soft penalties that trade off against obstacle clearance.
